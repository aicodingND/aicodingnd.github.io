01 / About
[ 01 / ABOUT ]

About

Who, and what they asked for

The member is a Geneva-based family with a New York office and a child in school on each side of the Atlantic. For most of the year, one parent or the other crosses the ocean every other week. They had flown charter for a decade and were tired of three things: a different aircraft each time, a different voice on the phone, and a price that moved with the weather.

They did not ask for a faster jet. They asked for one fewer decision. The brief, in their words, was to make the crossing feel like a commute that someone else already knew the shape of. We proposed the Meridian membership with a standing Teterboro to Geneva pattern, the heavy cabin held against their calendar, and a single Flight Team contact who would learn the rhythm.

02 / Context
[ 02 / CONTEXT ]

Context

Why a pattern, not a flight

A recurring crossing is where charter quietly fails. Each leg is quoted alone, so the price drifts and the aircraft is whatever is repositioning nearby. The family was paying for novelty they never wanted. A membership lets us do the opposite. Because the pattern is known, the Flight Team can plan repositioning ahead, hold the same heavy cabin, and keep the rate fixed for the term.

Over the season the standing pattern ran to 41 segments, all on the heavy cabin, all with the same two-person Flight Team and the same Cabin Host profile. The average call-out fell to under nine hours because the slots were already drafted. The novelty went out of the journey, which was the point. The crossing became a thing the family no longer had to hold in their heads.
